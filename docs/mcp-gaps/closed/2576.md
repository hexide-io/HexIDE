# No call turned the mouse wheel, and scroll said a control drawing its own bar had nothing to scroll — **CLOSED** (#2576, 2026-10-28)

**Symptom (2026-10-28, Windows at 150%, #2476's live check).** A running form held TreeView1 with 30 nodes and its vertical bar showing.
- **The bar was there.** `inspect_element` read the TreeView's own bar, a child it draws, as `range: {value: 0, minimum: 0, maximum: 26}`.
- **scroll said otherwise.** `interact(target: "Window/None[TreeView1]", action: "scroll", value: "line_down", window: "Form1")` replied that "neither 'VBTreeView[TreeView1]' nor anything containing it has content taller than its viewport". That read as a fact about the control, and it was false: `scroll` looks for a ScrollViewer or a container that scrolls, and never considered a bar the control owns.
- **Nothing sent a wheel notch.** `scroll`, even where it works, moves a scroller's offset or a bar's value. It raises no `PointerWheelChanged`, so it can't show what a wheel does: the rows per notch, the selection staying put, the stop at the ends.

**Fixed (#2576).**
- **`interact` has a `wheel` action.** Its value is a direction, a notch count and an optional point, as `down 3`.
  - It sends one wheel event a notch through the window's input, at the point or at the centre of what shows of the target, so the control's own handling runs.
  - It waits for a drawn frame first, as `click` does, since a wheel is hit-tested against what was drawn.
  - The reply names what the wheel reached and what moved, before and after: the scroller `scroll` would use, and any bar the control draws itself. When nothing moved, it says so, and that the control's handlers ran all the same.
- **`scroll`'s refusal says what it looked for,** rather than claiming the content fits. Where the control draws a bar of its own along that axis, it names the bar, its path under the target and its position, with `set_range_value` and `wheel` as the routes that move it.
- **Guarded by** `InteractWheelTests`: a ScrollViewer both ways, the stop at the top, a control that draws its own bar, the refusal, and a bad value.
